    \section{Quadrupole Scanner}
    A quadrupole scanner can be used to measure the transverse emittance of the electron beam to a high accuracy. This is done by conforming to the fact that the beam optics can be described in terms of transfer matrices and if the distribution of the beam after the optics is known in position space, the entire phase space can be reconstructed by taking multiple measurements and finding the solutions by solving multiple equations. 
    
    
    In this case, we will use three quadrupoles along the path of the beam and a beam profile monitor after an optimal drift length. By varying the quadrupole strength and collecting multiple measurements, we can reconstruct the transverse phase space and the emittance of the beam. This method of quadrupole scanning technique is called multiple-optics single-location scheme\cite{PhysRevSTAB.17.052801}.  
    
    \subsection{Theory}
    
    The transverse statistical properties which can be described entirely by the phase space elements can be written in the form of a 4D transverse matrix given by:
    
    \begin{equation}
        \sigma^{4D} = \begin{pmatrix} \langle x^2\rangle &  \langle xx'\rangle& \langle xy\rangle &  \langle xy'\rangle\\  \langle xx'\rangle &  \langle x'^2\rangle & \langle x'y\rangle &  \langle x'y'\rangle\\ \langle xy\rangle &  \langle x'y\rangle& \langle y^2\rangle &  \langle yy'\rangle\\ \langle xy'\rangle &  \langle x'y'\rangle& \langle yy'\rangle &  \langle y'^2\rangle\end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix}\sigma_{xx}&\sigma_{xy}\\\sigma_{xy}^T & \sigma_{yy} \end{pmatrix}
    \end{equation}
    
    Where $x$ and $y$ are horizontal and vertical coordinates and $x'$ and $y'$ are their derivatives with respect to the longitudinal coordinate. In the ideal case when $x$ and $y$ coordinates are decoupled, $\sigma_{xy} = 0$, and only measuring the 2D beam matrices would suffice to charecterize the transverse emittance of the beam. But in reality there will be some $xy$ correlations and the $\sigma_{xy} \neq 0$. If we tend to ignore the $\sigma_{xy}$ terms and only measure the 2D transverse emittances, then the obtained emittances are called \textit{apparent} emittances. 
    
    In the case of charecterization of the electron gun, we begin with the assumption of the gun being a black box and hence cannot rely on the accuracy of the apparent emittances. Therefore we need to measure the entire 4D matrix. 
    
    As the beam progresses along the quadrupole triplet, the transverse matrix changes according to the following relation: 
    
    \begin{equation}
        \sigma_{s} = R \sigma_{s_{0}} R^T
    \end{equation}
    
    Where R is the transport matrix between $s$ and $s_0$ whose elements are determined by the quadrupole strengths, length and the drift space. 
    
    Once the $R$ matrix and $\sigma_s$ are known, $\sigma_{s_0}$ can be reconsctructed by taking atleast 4 measurements
